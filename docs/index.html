User guide

Export tables from Confluence to Excel or CSV in three steps.

Export tables in three steps

  1. Open a Confluence page that contains tables. Tables inside expand sections and page layouts are found too.
  2. Select ••• (top right) → Apps → Export tables (Excel/CSV).
  3. Check the preview, then select the Export button at the bottom right.

Excel

  • All tables are selected by default. Select a table in the list to leave it out, and select it again to put it back. The circle on the thumbnail shows whether a table is included.
  • The file contains one sheet per table. The sheet name comes from the title of the expand section or the heading above the table. The characters [ ] : * ? / \ are replaced, names are shortened to 31 characters (the Excel limit), and duplicate names get (2), (3) and so on. Tables without a name are called "Table 1", "Table 2" and so on, in the language of the app window (English, German, French, Spanish or Japanese).
  • Merged cells stay merged. Line breaks, special characters and emoji are kept.
  • Formulas are never executed: a cell like =SUM(A1:A2) is exported as text.
  • Numbers become real Excel numbers. The app reads them in your own Confluence language: in German for example 1,5, 1.234,56 and 12,5 %, in English 1,234.56. The decimals you see are kept. How Excel then displays them follows the regional settings of the computer that opens the file, so a colleague abroad may see a dot where you see a comma.
  • These values stay text: leading zeros (00123), -0, whole numbers with more than 11 digits (such as article or account numbers), currencies and dates. A date like 01.02.2026 is never turned into a number, and neither is a value with a trailing zero such as 1.10. A plain 1.1, however, is a decimal number in English and is treated as one: the app cannot tell a chapter number from a decimal, because nothing in the text distinguishes them. If a column holds chapter numbers, give one cell a trailing zero or a letter, and the whole column stays text

    Troubleshooting

    Troubleshooting
    You seeWhat to do
    "No tables on this page"Only real Confluence tables are exported. Tables inside images, attachments or embedded files cannot be read, and tables loaded by macros (for example Jira issue lists) are not included.
    "Could not read this page"You may not have permission to view the page, or Confluence is temporarily unavailable. Select Try again. If the page is clearly visible to you, your organisation may block apps in this space with a data security policy; ask your Confluence administrator whether an app access rule covers it.
    Everything lands in one column when you open a CSV fileExcel uses the list separator of your computer. Switch the separator in the app (; or ,) and export again, or open the file through Data > From Text/CSV.
    You need a PDFExport to Excel and print to PDF from there, or use Confluence's own Export to PDF for the whole page.
    "License not active"Ask your Confluence administrator to renew the app.
    The menu item is missingThe app license may be inactive, or the app is not installed on this site.
    The download does not startAllow downloads for your Confluence site in your browser. If the app shows a Save file button (for example in Safari), select it to start the download.

    For administrators

    Who we are. Table Export for Confluence is built and supported by TooSolv Studio, a small independent software studio based in Germany. Contact details and postal address are in our legal notice.

    What the app can reach. One permission only: read:page:confluence. The app reads the page the user has open, as that user, with that user's own permissions. It cannot search your site, list or read other pages, reach a whole space, or open attachments. It has no backend, no servers and no storage, so there is nothing for us to see and nothing for anyone to breach.

    Does an export app make it easier to take data out? It is a fair question, so here is the precise answer. Everything the app can read, the user can already see on screen, select, copy and paste into Excel, or take out with Confluence's own Export to Word and Export to PDF. The app makes that route cleaner, not wider. If your policy is that certain content must not leave Confluence, the controls that work are the ones you already have: page and space permissions, and Atlassian's data security policies, which can block this app in named spaces.

    Restricting the app. You can block it in individual spaces with Atlassian's data security policies (Administration, Security, Data security policies, App access rules). You can also uninstall it per site.

    Audit. The app writes no log and keeps no record of who exported which page, because it stores nothing at all. If you need such a record, use Confluence's own audit log and page analytics; the app reads pages exactly the way the user does. Forge gives app vendors access to the app logs of the sites where their app is installed, and that sharing is on by default. This app writes no log output at all, so there is nothing in those logs for us to see. Your administrator can switch log sharing off at any time at admin.atlassian.com under Apps, Connected apps.

    What we do count. The app keeps eight plain counters so we can tell whether it is working: for example that a page was read, that an Excel file was created, or that the app hit an error. They carry no content, no names and no page identifiers, and all eight are listed on our privacy page. Atlassian's platform adds which site a count came from and roughly when, never which user or which page. To switch them off for your whole site: go to admin.atlassian.com, select the site, select Apps, then Connected apps, choose this app, and in the Details tab turn off "Custom metrics access".

    Certification. We hold no SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certificate. Both certify how a vendor runs its servers and handles customer data. We run no servers and receive no customer data: the app runs entirely inside Atlassian's Forge platform, which is itself covered by Atlassian's certifications. Our security page explains the setup in full.

    Billing. Atlassian bills Marketplace apps by the number of users on your Confluence site, not by the number of people who use the app. That is how the Marketplace works for every app, ours included. Sites with 10 users or fewer pay nothing.

    Trial and cancellation. Atlassian gives every paid app a free trial; its length and terms are set by Atlassian and shown on the pricing tab of our listing. When a licence ends, the menu entry disappears on the same day and the app can do nothing at all. Files your people already downloaded stay on their devices, because they are ordinary Excel and CSV files with no link back to us. There is nothing to delete and nothing to hand back, because we never held anything.

    Privacy

    The page is read in your browser with your own permissions. No page content is sent to or stored by the vendor, and the app stores nothing in your browser. We receive only a handful of plain counters that say an export ran, never what was in it. See the privacy policy.

    If you select Help in an error message, your browser opens this documentation page, which is hosted by GitHub. Nothing about your Confluence page is sent with it.

    Open source

    The app window contains software by other authors, used under their licences: iframe-resizer and lodash (MIT), tslib (0BSD), and Atlassian's own Forge bridge packages. The full notices are in the file third-party.txt that ships with the app.

    Moving from another table export app

    If your current table export app is built on Atlassian Connect, your admin may have started seeing warnings about it. What Connect end of support means, how to check your site, and what a move costs explains the timeline with links to Atlassian's announcements. Short version: for an export tool there is no data to migrate, because your tables stay in Confluence.
